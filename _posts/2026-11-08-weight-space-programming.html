  • Weight-space programming
    • Weights are the numbers inside a neural network. Training usually sets them with gradient descent on data.
    • Weight-space programming lets an LLM read another network's weights and write new values itself, without gradient descent or an optimizer.

  • Example: a car that cannot climb a ramp
    • A network trained for 1 billion steps drives the car straight at the ramp and stalls, in all 256 tests. To jump the red strip and reach the blue box, the car has to back up first.
    • GPT-6 Astra read the weights and, in one reply with no tools, added a small path that backs the car up and then hands control back to the trained network. It changed 3.5% of the weights, and the car succeeded in all 1,024 tests.

  • Example: an ink wash prompt that paints like Lee Jung-seop
    • AI Frontier asked GPT-6 Astra to edit the weights of an image model so that the prompt an ink wash painting of a bull comes out in the style of six paintings by Lee Jung-seop. Training and prompt changes were not allowed.
    • In Fig. 2, each box is one block of weights, and the gray path leads from the prompt to the painting. Red boxes changed at the selected step, and step 0 is the original model. The chosen edit changes 0.40% of the weights and none of the faint blue 4-bit boxes, which hold 95.8% of them.
    • At step 20, Astra's choice, the similarity to the six paintings reached 0.776, above a prompt that describes the style in words (0.739). Astra still noted that the result is not Lee Jung-seop.
